The Week Hollywood and the UN Share a Calendar

UN High-Level Week, Global Citizen and the VMAs all land in the same seven days — on opposite coasts. The industry is treating them as separate calendars. They are not.

PR STARPOWER Press Room

For one week at the end of September, the entertainment industry's attention will be split across three thousand miles — and almost nobody is planning for it as a single event.

The United Nations General Assembly convenes its High-Level Week in New York from 22 to 28 September. Climate Week NYC runs 20 to 27 September. Global Goals Week runs 19 to 27. The Global Citizen Festival lands on the Great Lawn in Central Park on Saturday 26 September, hosted by Hugh Jackman, with Lauryn Hill and Wyclef Jean marking thirty years of The Score, alongside Lenny Kravitz, John Legend, Lainey Wilson, Shaboozey and TOMORROW X TOGETHER.

And on Sunday 27 September, the MTV Video Music Awards return to Los Angeles for the first time since 2017, live from the Peacock Theater on CBS, MTV and Paramount+.

Two coasts. One week. The same talent pool, the same publicists, the same photographers — and a choice that has to be made by early September.

Why this is not a scheduling footnote

The industry files these under different calendars. Awards coverage handles the VMAs. Global affairs desks handle UNGA. Music press handles Global Citizen. Nobody owns the overlap, because the overlap does not belong to any single beat.

But the people who make these weeks work — the talent, the teams around them, the photographers, the brand activations that follow attention — cannot be in two places. An artist who wants the humanitarian platform in New York on Saturday and the pop-culture platform in Los Angeles on Sunday has a logistics problem and a positioning problem at the same time.

The positioning problem is the more interesting one. These are two entirely different kinds of visibility. Global Citizen and the UN week confer a seriousness that a red carpet cannot buy. The VMAs confer a currency that humanitarian work does not. Historically, careers that have managed both have done so deliberately and over years, not by accident in a single weekend.

What this actually means for anyone with something to say

Three things follow from it.

The credential is the constraint, not the invitation. UN High-Level Week is not a red carpet with a longer guest list. Access runs through accreditation, missions, and side-event programming — a system that has nothing to do with entertainment publicity and does not respond to it. Firms without standing in that world will find the door does not open, regardless of who their client is.

The satellite calendar is where the room actually is. The General Debate is closed. The festival is ticketed by action, not sale. What surrounds them — receptions, side events, delegation dinners, the Climate Week programme — is where conversations happen and where a well-placed guest is genuinely welcome. That calendar is not published in one place, which is exactly why it rewards the people who track it.

The decision window is now, not then. Accreditation, side-event programming and delegation lists close weeks ahead. VMA carpet arrangements are locked by teams and publicists well before the broadcast. Anyone still deciding in mid-September has already chosen by default.

The Los Angeles return matters on its own

Set the New York week aside and the VMAs alone are a significant West Coast event. The show has been held in New York and New Jersey since 2017. Its return brings the full apparatus back to Los Angeles for a weekend — the pre-parties, the after-parties, the gifting, the photographers, the brand activations that attach themselves to a broadcast of that size.

Nominations were announced on 18 August. Madonna leads with eleven, followed by Taylor Swift with nine, and Ariana Grande and Sabrina Carpenter with seven each.

That top line is worth sitting with. The most-nominated artist at the 2026 VMAs began releasing records in 1982. We have written elsewhere about the commercial return of the legacy artist; this is the clearest data point yet that the audience has not moved on from the people who built the form.
